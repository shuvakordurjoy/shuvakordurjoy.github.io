Abstract

The growing global demand for faded, vintage, and rugged visual effects has increased the importance of washing processes in knit apparel manufacturing. Conventional washing techniques, particularly acid washing, are widely used to achieve such effects. However, these processes consume large amounts of water and chemicals and contribute significantly to environmental pollution and waste generation. This experimental study presents a comparative analysis of ozone washing and conventional acid washing applied to knit apparel (100% cotton Knit Denim, Single Lacoste, and Double Lacoste). The comparison is conducted based on quality-related parameters, including aesthetic performance, ISO 105 colorfastness, and GSM properties, as well as environmental indicators such as water consumption, chemical usage, energy consumption, and Jeanologia EIM v2.0 environmental impact score. The findings demonstrate that ozone washing delivers comparable or improved garment quality with 40%–45% less water, 45%–64.71% less chemical consumption, and a 42%–71% lower EIM environmental impact score.

B.SC. THESIS RESEARCH · SUPERVISOR: SHAFAT AHMED BIN KAMAL

“COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS OF OZONE WASH OVER CONVENTIONAL ACID WASH FOR KNIT APPAREL”

This academic research project presents an experimental comparative analysis of gas-phase ozone washing technology (Jeanologia G2 Lab) and conventional potassium permanganate acid washing (Tupesa Stone 75) applied to 100% cotton Knit Denim (221 GSM), Single Lacoste (215 GSM), and Double Lacoste (230 GSM) knit apparel.

Evaluated across ISO 105 colorfastness standards, GSM dimensional stability, resource consumption, and Jeanologia EIM v2.0 ecological scoring, ozone washing achieved superior rubbing fastness on Knit Denim while reducing water use by 40%–45%, chemical consumption by 45%–64.71%, and Total EIM Impact Score by 42%–71%.
